Why this coach

There is a reason it is the one you see outside the arena.

Professional sports teams, touring musicians and corporate groups specify the H3-45 by name. It is the top of the North American coach market, and it is what we put your group on.

Prevost rebuilt this coach from the nose back: a curved forward profile worth up to 12% better fuel economy, a cabin roughly 50% quieter in the driver area, an all-new window layout with fewer obstructions, and a wider curved entrance stair lit three times brighter than the coach it replaced. Every H3-45 we run is owned by Campbell, serviced in our own Slippery Rock shop, and driven by an operator we know by name.

Passenger experience

What your group finds when the door opens

Twelve hours is a long time to sit anywhere. Most of what follows exists so that nobody notices the last four.

Cloud One seating

Prevost’s current seat: deep recline, real lumbar support, three-point belts at every position, and a footrest. It is the difference between arriving rested and arriving sore.

Power at every row

110V household outlets with USB. Phones, laptops and tablets all charge, so nobody spends the trip rationing battery.

An all-new window layout

Redesigned for fewer obstructions and better sightlines. Frameless thermopane glass keeps the cabin quiet and the temperature even.

Customisable interior lighting

RGB cabin lighting in six colours, plus dedicated LED reading lamps. Set the mood for a wedding shuttle or dim it for an overnight run.

A quieter cabin

Aerodynamic redesign cut wind noise in the driver area by nearly half. Conversation at normal volume, front to back.

Onboard lavatory

Standard on every H3-45 in our fleet. Long legs of the trip do not have to be planned around rest stops.

Redesigned entry

A wider, curved entranceway with an updated handrail and 300% brighter lighting. Boarding fifty-six people at night stops being a bottleneck.

Full climate control

A two-ton auxiliary compressor and a 100,000 BTU auxiliary heater, with improved air distribution and passenger controls overhead.

Parcel racks and monitors

New full-length overhead racks, monitors with DVD, and a PA with a tour-guide microphone for group leaders.

Driver area & safety

The parts your group never sees

A comfortable coach that stops on the shoulder is not a comfortable coach. Most of the engineering on a current-model H3-45 is aimed at that.

A luggage bay door indicator

A dash indicator pinpoints any baggage door left unsecured. Small feature, and it prevents exactly the sort of incident nobody wants to explain.

Full-colour instrument cluster

One centralised digital view of the whole coach, so the operator is reading information rather than hunting for it.

Integrated camera and GPS

Surround view, back-up view and navigation all on the entertainment screen. Placing a 45-foot coach in a tight hotel forecourt gets considerably safer.

Improved forward visibility

Narrower front corner pillars, two long wiper blades, and wider, more reliable driver blinds. Aerodynamic mirrors with optional eMirrors.

An ergonomic driver station

A fully adjustable steering column with fingertip controls and a powerful dedicated driver-area HVAC. A less fatigued driver is a safety system.

FMVSS-compliant seat rails

The welded seat rail system meets the FMVSS/210 static pull test, which is what makes three-point belts at every seat mean something.

LED headlights

Rated beyond 60,000 hours. Night driving on unlit two-lane roads is most of what a charter coach actually does.

Serviced by a Prevost authorized service center

Ours. The shop on Grove City Road holds Prevost’s own authorization, and it is where Millennium Luxury Coaches and Florida Coaches send their equipment.

A replacement coach if it matters

If a coach has trouble on the road, we send another one. A mechanical problem does not end your trip.

Powertrain & storage

Why the mechanical side shows up in your quote

You are not buying an engine. But what is under the floor decides what the trip costs, how smooth the ride is, and whether the coach shows up.

A smoother ride on a long grade

The Volvo D13 makes its 435 horsepower with best-in-class torque low in the rev range, matched to a fifth-generation Allison automatic. On a Pennsylvania mountain grade that means the coach holds speed without hunting for gears — which passengers feel as a steady ride rather than a laboring one.

Up to 12% better fuel economy

Prevost redesigned the body for airflow, and fuel is one of the largest line items in any charter price. Fewer gallons burned on your itinerary is money that does not show up in your quote.

Long legs between stops

222 gallons of fuel, with filler necks on both sides. The coach is not detouring to find a truck stop that can take it, and your schedule stays yours.

Traction when the weather turns

Automatic tag axle unload shifts weight onto the drive axle for grip when it is needed, and lifts it back for tyre wear when it is not. Pennsylvania in February is the reason this matters.

Maintained in our own shop

We are a Prevost authorized service center. Tyres, brakes, and scheduled service are handled here rather than farmed out to whoever has an opening — so the coach spends its time in service, not waiting on someone else's bay.

If something goes wrong, we have another

We run a fleet, not a single coach. A mechanical problem on the road means a replacement is dispatched, not a cancelled trip and a refund.

What the numbers mean

What the H3-45 means for your group

Manufacturers publish specifications. Here is what those specifications actually change about your trip.

56seats

Nobody rides in a van behind the coach

A full high school football roster with coaches and trainers goes on one vehicle. Two 28-seat minibuses cost more, arrive at different times, and split your group across two drivers. One coach, one arrival, one point of contact.

460cubic feet

Every bag, every instrument case, still fits

This is the largest underfloor volume in the class. In practice: a suitcase per passenger plus a marching band’s cases, or a full set of team equipment and medical bags, with room left over. You will not be putting luggage on seats. Bay doors open on both sides, so loading works no matter which way the coach is parked.

110Vat every row

Arrive with a charged phone, a finished paper, and an edited film session

Standard outlets, not just USB, at every row — so laptops, camera batteries, and game-film tablets all work. On a six-hour ride this is the difference between productive time and dead time.

Yesrestroom

Fewer stops, earlier arrival

An onboard lavatory means the driver stops when the schedule calls for it, not every time somebody needs a rest area. On a long haul that is often an hour saved, and it is the single most common reason groups upgrade from a school bus.

Seated above the traffic, not in it

The H3-45 puts passengers a deck above the driver. You look over the guardrail instead of through it, the ride is noticeably smoother over the rear axle, and road noise in the cabin drops. It is the same coach touring musicians and professional sports teams ride, for the same reasons.

3-pointbelts, every seat

Reclining seats built for all-day travel

Prevost Cloud One seating — deep recline, real legroom, three-point belts at every seat, and individual reading lamps. Climate control holds the cabin steady whether it is August in Virginia or January in Erie.
