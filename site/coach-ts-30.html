Why this coach

Stop paying to move empty seats.

Booking a 56-passenger coach for a group of thirty means chartering twenty-six seats nobody sits in. The TS 30 is the same standard of coach at the size the trip actually calls for.

Temsa built the TS 30 on a monocoque integral stainless steel structure — the same construction approach as a full-size coach, engineered for a service life beyond ten years, with an American Cummins and Allison driveline. It is a genuine motorcoach that happens to be short, not a small bus with coach trim. And it goes places a 45-footer physically cannot.

Passenger experience

To those who do not accept compromises

Shorter coach, same expectations. Nothing on this list was left off because the body is thirty-one feet instead of forty-five.

34 + 1 reclining seats

Wide, properly reclining coach seats with real leg-room — not commuter shuttle seating. Three-point belts at every passenger position.

An onboard restroom

A recirculating lavatory with a twelve-gallon tank. Included, not optional, which is unusual at this size.

USB-A and USB-C at every seat

Dual outlets per seat, so modern and older devices both charge without anyone hunting for an adapter.

Full automatic HVAC

Up to 87,430 BTU of cooling and 55,000 BTU of heating. High-capacity for the cabin volume, so it holds temperature on a July afternoon.

Ambient LED cabin lighting

Warm ambient lighting throughout the cabin rather than the flat white of a transit bus.

Ventilated roof hatches

Fresh air on top of the HVAC, and a second means of egress.

Wide ergonomic entrance steps

Broad, well-lit steps. It matters most with a group of students, or with anyone who does not climb stairs easily.

Ample overhead racks

Full-length parcel racks above the seats, on top of the underfloor bays.

Tempered laminated glazing

Laminated side windows and a rear window, with distinctive flooring and interior trim.

Driver area & safety

Advanced safety systems, standard

Temsa specifies the TS 30 with the full Bendix stability package. It is the same class of electronics you would find on a full-size coach.

Bendix ESP stability control

Electronic Stability Control, ABS and Automatic Traction Control as standard. Confident handling in rain, snow and on a crowned back road.

Three-point belts throughout

Every passenger seat, plus an ISRI air-ride driver seat with its own three-point belt.

Monocoque integral structure

100% stainless steel structural construction, engineered for a service life beyond ten years. Corrosion is what kills coaches in Pennsylvania winters.

Digital cluster with rearview

A high-resolution instrument cluster with an integrated rearview display, so the operator is not turning to look.

Improved driver visibility

A redesigned driver window and a refined mirror arm — both aimed at the sightlines that matter in tight urban placement.

Cruise control and battery protection

Cruise control, low-voltage warning and a battery circuit separator. Fewer ways for a long day to go wrong.

Air suspension

A well-configured air suspension system with robust multiplex electrics, tuned for both urban and highway work.

Serviced in our own shop

Preventive maintenance on a set schedule on Grove City Road. AC service, tyre work and windshield repair handled on site.

A replacement coach if it matters

If a coach has trouble on the road, we send another one. A mechanical problem does not end your trip.

Powertrain & storage

Cummins and Allison, and 35% more luggage than you expect

An American driveline in a European-built body, and a baggage compartment Temsa expanded by more than a third.

Cummins B6.7 diesel

Six cylinders, 6.7 litres, EPA 24 compliant. 250 hp with 660 lb-ft of torque at 1,600 rpm — low-end pull where a loaded coach needs it.

Allison B300 transmission

Fully automatic, matched to the Cummins, and specified to improve fuel economy over the coach it replaced.

190 cubic feet of baggage

Underfloor bays, expanded 35% over the previous TS 30. Genuine coach luggage capacity, not a rear boot.

27 ft 5 in turning radius

Best-in-class manoeuvrability. This is the specification that decides whether a coach can actually reach your venue.

80 gallons of fuel

Sized to the coach. A smaller vehicle burning less fuel is a real line item in your quote.

28,439 lb GVWR

96.5 inches wide and eleven feet tall on a 185-inch wheelbase — it fits under canopies a full-size coach cannot clear.

Continental 265/70R 19.5

On 19.5-inch Alcoa wheels.

Low fuel consumption

The point of a right-sized coach. Fewer gallons per mile for the same group, same standard of ride.

Built for both jobs

Temsa engineered it for urban and highway duty in one vehicle, which is exactly the mix a charter week looks like.
